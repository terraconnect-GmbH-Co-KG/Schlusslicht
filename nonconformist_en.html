The piece of the day

Don't Be a Taillight

A well-meant piece of advice. Read it once, straight through. Then, when you reach the bottom, read it again — back to front.

Don't fall behind. That's the first rule, and for a long time it sounds like the only one you need. Whoever keeps up front gets seen; whoever drops back gets caught, passed, sorted out. So hold the pace. Wear what everyone wears. Think what pays. Smile in the direction everyone is already smiling — then you never have to explain why. There's a lane they call the fast lane, and it wasn't built for you to look at. It was built for you to stay in.

The people up front know how it's done, after all. They get up earlier, optimise the sleep they sacrifice for it, and call the exhaustion afterwards discipline. They turned life into a race and the race into a career, and because everyone is running, no one asks where to. The goal is to be up front. Up front of what is a question for those already left behind.

Take a role model. The phrase says it plainly: a model, placed in front of you, so you don't have to look for yourself. A role model spares you the hardest work of all — forming your own picture of things. You only have to follow it. And "to follow" is more honest than it pretends to be: it means to come after, to walk a path someone else has already chosen. It's obedience with better PR.

Whoever only ever follows has never thought a single thought through to the end. He borrowed it, and gave it back on time.

And the ones at the back? The slow, the awkward, the ones who stop where everyone else hurries on? They're the warning you show the children. See — not like that. The last isn't a person; he's a threat with an address. We built entire league tables just to make clear who he is. We call it competition when we organise it, and fate when we look away.

✳ ✳ ✳

So much for the advice. Now the point where it turns.

Because I'm not writing this from the front. I'm writing it from the back, by the red light this magazine is named after — the Schlusslicht, the taillight. And from here, the same landscape looks different.

A taillight isn't the loser of the convoy. It's the only lamp that faces backward. Everyone else beams forward, into a future they think they already know; the taillight is the only one checking whether someone is still there, falling behind. It keeps the whole column from piling into each other in the dark. You could say it lags behind. You could also say it's the only thing still looking back.

The fastest doesn't see the most. The last one sees everyone. He's the only one no one runs away from.

Whoever races at the very front has empty road ahead and everyone else behind — and never sees his own back. Whoever walks at the very back has the whole field in view: every gap, everyone who stumbles, everyone who falls out of line because they can't go on, or won't. The overview the winners claim for themselves belongs, in truth, to the last. They just have the faster view of the wall.

✳ ✳ ✳

They'll tell you this is only comfort for those who didn't make it. A pretty story for falling behind. But look closely at who says it — and you'll see it's always the people who live off the front. The ranking is never neutral. Someone decided what gets measured and what doesn't, and, strangely, it's never the things the slow are good at: care, the detour, doubt, the ability to stand still when standing still is the wiser move. We call it progress, but it's only the direction the fastest happen to be running.

Nonconformist doesn't mean running the other way on principle. That would only be obedience with the sign flipped — the contrarian still follows the herd, he just trails it in order to disagree. Nonconformist is whoever takes back the question that was taken from him: where to, exactly, and why, and who decided that. It's the most uncomfortable kind of freedom, because it has no lane to stay in.

So, the advice from the start, once more, now in plain words: "Don't be a taillight" means "don't look back." It means: run along, don't ask, and if someone falls behind you, at least you had the speed. It's the most polite command a society knows when it no longer wants to carry anyone.

We named ourselves after the red light. After the lamp that looks back. After the place nobody wants — and from which you see the most.

And that's the real reason only one piece a day stands here from now on. No stream, no feed, no three items to swipe past. So that you read it to the end. And then, if you like, once more — from the last line to the first. You'll notice the advice from the beginning sounds different the second time. It hasn't changed. Only your angle has.

Read it back to front. That's all we ever do here.

Why only one piece a day?
